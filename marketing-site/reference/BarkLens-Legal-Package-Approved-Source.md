### 1.3 What BarkLens is, stated precisely

**1.3.1 The two things the Service does.** BarkLens does two things, and it is important that you understand both of them exactly.

**First, it holds and organizes your dog's history.** You photograph or upload the documents you already have — veterinary records and lab reports, prescription and supplement labels, food packaging and guaranteed-analysis panels, product and treat packaging, and photographs of your dog's stool — and BarkLens reads them, extracts the values printed on them, and arranges them into a chart that grows over time. Every fact in that chart carries a pointer back to the document it came from, or an explicit statement that you entered it yourself. The chart is a record-keeping and preparation tool. Answers become more specific as your dog's chart fills, because a question asked with a lab report on file can be answered against the numbers on that report, while the same question asked with nothing on file can only be answered in general terms.

## 5. Cookie and Analytics Decision

**Effective: [Effective: ___, 2026]**
*This section is drafted as an internal recommendation to counsel and management, followed by the disclosure language we propose to publish. The recommendation portion is not intended for publication.*

### 5.1 Recommendation: yes, publish a short standalone Cookie Policy

**We recommend a short, standalone Cookie Policy in addition to the cookie paragraphs in the Privacy Policy.** Reasoning:

1. **The mobile app and the marketing website have different tracking surfaces.** The app uses SDKs and device identifiers, not cookies; barklens.com uses cookies and page tags. One document trying to describe both produces language that is wrong about each. A short cookie document scoped to the website is clearer and easier to keep accurate.
2. **Consent-banner mechanics need a place to live.** A banner needs a linked document that lists categories, names the specific cookies, states durations, and explains how to change a choice. Burying that in a long Privacy Policy makes the banner's "learn more" link nearly useless.
3. **Maintenance.** Cookie inventories change whenever marketing adds a tag. A standalone document can be updated without re-versioning the Privacy Policy.
4. **Cost is low.** A one-page policy plus a cross-reference from §2.3.10 is inexpensive to draft and maintain.

**Recommended structure:** Privacy Policy keeps a two-paragraph summary and a link; the Cookie Policy carries the table of specific cookies, purposes, durations, and the consent-management instructions.

**5.1.1 The Cookie Policy is also a launch control.** The published Cookie Policy is not only a disclosure; it is the artifact that demonstrates the company knows what its own product loads. For each technology it must identify the provider, the domain, the purpose, the category, the duration, whether the technology is first- or third-party, and how the user controls it. **The table must match actual network traffic and device storage in the shipped build. A generic cookie table copied from another site is worse than no table, because it is an inaccurate public statement about the company's own data flows.** [COUNSEL CONFIRM: complete a production cookie, SDK, pixel, local-storage, and identifier inventory immediately before launch and again after each material release, and confirm whether PostHog, Amplitude, neither, or another analytics SDK is present in the app.]

### 5.2 Where a consent banner is required, and what we recommend

**5.2.1 The legal picture.** Under the EU ePrivacy regime and UK law, non-essential cookies require prior informed consent, and a banner with genuine reject-all functionality is effectively mandatory. In the United States, no state law requires a cookie banner as such, but several state privacy laws require an opt-out of sale/sharing and targeted advertising and require honoring opt-out preference signals such as Global Privacy Control, and some states require consent (or a limitation right) for the processing of sensitive categories.

**5.2.2 Our situation.** BarkLens launches in the United States only, does not run advertising trackers, and does not sell or share personal information. That materially reduces banner exposure — **but the marketing website is publicly reachable from anywhere**, including the EEA and the UK, and analytics cookies set on an EU visitor's device without consent are the classic enforcement fact pattern.

**5.2.3 Recommendation: a geo-gated banner.**

- **EEA, UK, and Switzerland visitors:** show a **prior-consent banner** with equally prominent "Accept all" and "Reject all" controls and a granular preferences link. Set **no** non-essential cookie and fire **no** analytics tag until an affirmative choice is made. Record the consent (or refusal) with a timestamp and the policy version.
- **United States visitors:** no blocking banner. Show a persistent, unobtrusive privacy link in the footer with cookie preferences, honor Global Privacy Control and other opt-out preference signals automatically, and state plainly that we do not sell or share personal information or run advertising trackers.
- **California specifically:** because we do not sell or share, no "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link is strictly required; we nonetheless recommend a "Your Privacy Choices" footer link for clarity and to make the honoring of opt-out signals visible.
- **Everywhere:** never use a cookie wall, never use pre-checked non-essential categories, never treat continued scrolling as consent, and make withdrawal of consent as easy as giving it.

[COUNSEL CONFIRM: whether management accepts the cost and complexity of geo-gating, or prefers the simpler and more conservative option of applying the prior-consent banner to all visitors worldwide. The conservative option costs some analytics fidelity in the U.S. and nothing else.]

**5.2.4 Reduce the exposed surface first.** Because the launch is United States–only, the company should geo-restrict account creation and app-store availability to the United States, so that the only foreign exposure is a marketing page rather than a signed-in service. Where the public website remains reachable in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom and any nonessential Tracking Technology is present, that technology must be blocked until the visitor gives valid opt-in consent, and the reject and granular-choice controls must be **as easy to use as acceptance**. The same approach applies to every other jurisdiction that requires prior consent.

### A.3 The chart-state ladder (why answers differ between users)

Answers branch on how much of a given dog's record exists. The same question receives five different answers depending on chart state, sharpening as the chart fills:

| Chart state | What is on file | What the answer can be |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Nothing on file | Full category teaching — general education only |
| **1** | Breed, age, weight | Published baseline for that population |
| **2** | Records on file, no veterinary instruction | The chart picks the question worth asking |
| **3** | A veterinary instruction, no number | Quantify the gap between the instruction and the record |
| **4** | A veterinary instruction with a number | Transcription plus arithmetic against that instruction |

Two consequences for the legal documents. First, **the same question can produce materially different output for two users**, which is why §1.3.1 explains the mechanism rather than promising a consistent answer. Second, **states 3 and 4 are transcription-and-arithmetic states, not advice states** — the number in the answer comes from the veterinarian's instruction or the product label, never from BarkLens. §1.5.2 is drafted to make that distinction explicit, because it is the single most likely place for a user to believe BarkLens told them a dose.

Related architecture facts: the chart is an **append-only event ledger with a provenance envelope on every fact** (who asserted it, how it was captured, what page it came from, effective versus recorded date, and what superseded it). **BarkLens never appears as the assertor of a clinical fact** — every chart statement traces to a clinic record, a lab report, a product label, an owner entry, an owner-confirmed suggestion, BarkLens-derived arithmetic, or a library citation. **"Unknown" is a written value, never a blank field.** Corrections append rather than overwrite, which is why §2.9 says corrections append and §6.1 retains superseded entries. Trend surfaces gate on same-laboratory and same-method comparability, and **a reference range belongs to the laboratory that printed it** — the basis for §1.7.4.

### A.4 Photo-capture scope (the five ways in)

1. **Poop** — stool scoring against the published Waltham/Purina faecal scoring chart, as a **suggest-and-confirm** score recorded as the owner's observation, never the app's finding, with the source chart named beneath it.
2. **Food** — the guaranteed-analysis panel as printed, with conversion to a dry-matter basis.
3. **Meds and supplements** — the label quoted as printed, including any label-required bloodwork or monitoring.
4. **Products** — treat and chew cautions quoted in full.
5. **Vet records** — every value kept with the reference range printed on its own report.

### A.6 Additional context that shapes the documents

- **Veterinary review is scoped to the library, by a named licensed veterinarian who acts as the final source-approval gate.** Sources are drawn from tiered publishers (Merck Veterinary Manual, professional-association guidelines and consensus statements, teaching hospitals, and peer-reviewed literature at the top tier), and consumer-encyclopedia, retail-clinic, and blog sources are rejected on sight. **Every claim is bound to a verbatim passage; nothing authored in-house ever enters an evidence field.** This is why §1.8.1 can be stated as a firm commitment while §1.8.2 must be equally firm in the other direction.
- **Answers are not individually reviewed.** The company's own copy rule is that **"vet-reviewed sources" must never be shortened to "vet-reviewed answers,"** on the ground that the shorter phrase implies per-response sign-off and creates both an FTC net-impression problem and a VCPR exposure. §1.8.2 codifies this, and counsel should add it to the marketing-review checklist.
- **The product is deliberately not a dose calculator.** Competitor products won search traffic with dose calculators; the company banned them. §1.5.2 reflects a product decision, not merely a disclaimer.
- **Records and export are permanently free by decision.** The company reversed an earlier plan to delete free-plan chat history after 60 days, on the view that paywalling the category's trust-killer is wrong. §3.8 and §3.9 encode that decision; if the business reverses it again, those sections change.
- **The free tier is measured in pages, not documents** — 125 pages in a 7-day window, with no document count and no monthly allowance.
- **Positioning is the handoff, not the diagnosis.** The product positions as "the dog health companion that shows its work," aimed at an owner managing a change in a dog roughly six or older, and the tagline is required to appear with its proof line ("every answer traces back to your dog's own records — and a published veterinary source you can tap to read"). Counsel reviewing advertising should note that the proof line is a substantiable mechanism claim and the tagline alone is not intended to stand.
- **Quality-of-life, palliative, hospice, and end-of-life material exists in the library and is served only on request.** The product never raises it first, never proactively surfaces a quality-of-life assessment, and never targets paid creative at cancer. The label is plain ("Cancer"). This matters for advertising review: an organic page answering a query the visitor typed is permitted; paid creative aimed at a dying dog is not.
- **Documents are read by automated systems with known error modes**, including absence-verdict false negatives and drift in source pages, and the company's own internal position is that a wrong quotation is more dangerous than a missing one. §1.7.2 and §1.8.3 are drafted to match that posture rather than to minimize it.
