The panel comes home with two columns circled, or just one, and search treats them as the same word: liver. Sometimes it upgrades that to failure before you have finished the drive.

They are not the same question.

ALT is found at high levels inside liver cells. When those cells are injured, ALT can leak into the blood. It is not a direct measure of how well the liver is functioning. A high ALT does not identify the cause of the injury or establish whether the liver is failing. ([Cornell eClinPath: ALT](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/liver-injury/alanine-aminotransferase/); [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals))

ALP is an enzyme found in the liver, bone, and other tissues. Its activity can increase when bile flow is reduced, during bone growth, or because of corticosteroid effects in dogs. Corticosteroids can come from the body or from prescribed medicines. That is why a prednisone start date can help your veterinarian interpret the result. ([Cornell eClinPath: ALP](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/cholestasis/alkaline-phosphatase/); [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals))

One value being elevated is a conversation starter. Both values elevating next to each other is a different situation. Neither one is a diagnosis.

## Two reports can print the same flags

ALT within that laboratory’s reference interval alongside an elevated ALP in a dog receiving prednisone is one example. Both enzymes increasing in a dog with reduced appetite is another. An elevated ALP in a growing puppy is a third. These are examples of context, not a way to decide the cause at home. A medication start date alone does not establish why a result changed, and ALT within the reference interval does not rule out serious liver disease. ([Cornell eClinPath: ALP](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/cholestasis/alkaline-phosphatase/); [Cornell eClinPath: ALT](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/liver-injury/alanine-aminotransferase/); [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals))

The page looks identical until you put the neighbor column and the start date next to it.

Clinics treat ALT as a leakage enzyme. A high result means its measured activity is above that laboratory’s reference interval. It does not identify a particular disease or establish whether food or treatment should change. ([Cornell eClinPath: ALT](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/liver-injury/alanine-aminotransferase/); [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals))

Veterinarians use ALP in assessing liver and bile-flow problems, while considering corticosteroid effects and other possible contributors. A high result means its measured activity is above that laboratory’s reference interval. It does not by itself establish a blocked bile duct or diagnose Cushing’s. ([Cornell eClinPath: ALP](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/cholestasis/alkaline-phosphatase/); [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals))

Your veterinarian may also look at GGT and bilirubin alongside ALP and ALT. Bilirubin is a pigment, not an enzyme. Some medications can affect liver enzyme activity, and bone growth can raise ALP. ([Cornell eClinPath: ALP](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/cholestasis/alkaline-phosphatase/); [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals); [Merck: Jaundice](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/pathophysiology-of-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/jaundice-in-small-animals)) You do not have to sort those possibilities at the kitchen table. Bring the pieces that help the veterinarian interpret them.

## The start date next to both numbers

This result. The date it was drawn. Whether a steroid or another long-term medicine was already on the list. Anything the house noticed about appetite, thirst, stool, or the color of the gums.

Include seizure medications and all other prescribed medicines, supplements, and any start dates or changes you know about. Some seizure medications can affect liver enzyme activity. Earlier panels can help the veterinarian evaluate how results have changed over time. ([Cornell eClinPath: ALT](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/liver-injury/alanine-aminotransferase/); [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals))

Do not stop or change a prescribed medicine because of this article. Contact the prescribing veterinarian with questions about the medicine or the result.

## What the house already knows

Abnormal enzyme results can appear on a health-screening panel before an owner notices a problem. ([Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals)) A dog looking well is not a reason to dismiss a flagged result; ask the veterinarian who ordered the panel what follow-up is appropriate.

When there is something, date it. Eating less. Vomiting. A yellow tint you are not sure about. A medicine that started between draws. You are handing the clinic a timeline, not a diagnosis.

Yellow gums, skin, or the whites of the eyes can be signs of jaundice, which has more than one possible underlying cause. ([Merck: Jaundice](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/pathophysiology-of-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/jaundice-in-small-animals)) If you notice yellow discoloration, your dog cannot keep water down, or your dog seems very weak, contact your veterinarian or an emergency clinic now. If your dog collapses, seek emergency veterinary care immediately. ([Cornell Emergency and Critical Care](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/hospitals/services/emergency-and-critical-care-0)) Do not wait for another enzyme result.

When you go back, bring both values, the medication list, any known start dates, earlier panels, and your observations. Let the veterinarian do the interpreting.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a high ALT mean liver failure?

Not by itself. ALT can increase with liver-cell injury, but it does not establish whether the liver is failing or rule out serious liver disease. Your veterinarian interprets it alongside the examination, history, other tests, and medications. ([Cornell eClinPath: ALT](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/liver-injury/alanine-aminotransferase/); [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals))

### Does a high ALP mean liver disease or Cushing’s?

Not by itself. ALP can rise for several reasons, including reduced bile flow, corticosteroid effects, and bone growth. The result alone neither confirms nor rules out liver disease or Cushing’s. ([Cornell eClinPath: ALP](https://eclinpath.com/chemistry/liver/cholestasis/alkaline-phosphatase/); [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals))

### What is a normal ALP or ALT in dogs?

Start with the reference interval printed on the report and ask your veterinarian how it applies to your dog. Do not import a range from another website. The reference interval is one part of interpretation, not a stand-alone diagnosis or a measure of liver function. ([Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/laboratory-analyses-and-imaging-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals/enzyme-activity-in-hepatic-disease-in-small-animals))
