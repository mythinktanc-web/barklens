What the markers measure

Blood glucose — a snapshot, and it can be raised by stress alone, which is common in a clinic.

One reading is not the diagnosis. Merck states that diagnosis is based on documentation of persistent hyperglycemia and glucosuria — persistent being the operative word.

Source · Merck — Diabetes Mellitus

Fructosamine — reflects average glucose over roughly the previous two to three weeks. It’s how vets separate a genuinely raised glucose from a stressed one.

It has a caveat worth knowing: eClinPath notes fructosamine concentrations may be falsely lower in severe low blood protein, although values appear unaffected when protein sits within reference intervals. Albumin belongs in the same view.

Source · Cornell eClinPath — Fructosamine

Glucose in urine — the kidneys spill glucose into urine once blood levels pass a threshold.

What’s read together

eClinPath states that any cause of hyperglycemia, transient or sustained, may result in glucose in the urine if concentrations are high enough to exceed the renal threshold, and that a positive dipstick for glucose should be interpreted alongside biochemical results and other evidence of tubular dysfunction.

Source · Cornell eClinPath — Urinalysis

So the working set is: blood glucose, fructosamine, urine glucose, and albumin — four values, three reports, usually different days.

eClinPath also lists Cushing’s disease among the causes of persistent hyperglycemia producing glucose in the urine, which is why the two conditions keep appearing on each other’s pages.

Monitoring, once he’s on insulin

The questions change. Vets look at glucose curves or continuous monitoring, fructosamine over time, weight, water intake, and how he’s actually doing day to day — which often matters more than any single value.

This is the condition where a running record earns its keep fastest, because the useful question is nearly always about the trend.

Questions worth asking your vet

·Could stress explain this glucose reading?
·Does the fructosamine agree with it?
·What should I be recording at home, and how often?
·Should his food and treats stay exactly the same?
·What would tell you the current dose isn’t right anymore?
