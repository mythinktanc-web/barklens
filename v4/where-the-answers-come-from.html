Where the answers come from.

Anything BarkLens tells you about your dog’s health comes from one of two places: your dog’s own records, or a verified veterinary source. Nowhere else.

The library

Every general claim traces to a published veterinary source — clinical practice guidelines, veterinary manuals, peer-reviewed journals. Not collected from the open web. Read, verified, and stored word-for-word alongside the page it came from.

That means when BarkLens explains what a marker measures, there is a specific document behind it, and you can see which one.

The sources behind these pages include the Merck Veterinary Manual, Cornell University’s eClinPath and Animal Health Diagnostic Center, AAHA practice guidelines, ACVIM consensus statements, ACVS, IRIS, and FDA-approved drug labeling.

The verification

Every stored passage is checked character-for-character against the original document. Not paraphrased and spot-checked — compared exactly.

When a source contradicts itself, or prints something ambiguously, the claim is held rather than published. Nothing reaches an answer on a maybe.

This is the part that makes BarkLens slower to build than a chatbot. It’s also why we can tell you where anything came from.

The review

A licensed veterinarian reviews the source library and the safety rules that govern every answer.

Not your dog’s results — those belong to your own vet, who has examined him and knows him. What’s reviewed here are the standards behind the answers: what BarkLens is allowed to say, what it must never say, and when it should tell you to make a call.

Your dog’s own records

Every value is read against the range printed on its own report. Every lab prints its own — the same value can sit inside one lab’s range and above another’s, and reading against a generic range is how people frighten themselves over nothing, or miss something real.

Every vet note is quoted exactly as written. Not summarized, not softened.

The record leads. The science explains.

Medications

The label asks for bloodwork. The chart answers.

Carprofen is one of the most common prescriptions in dogs — and its own label asks for bloodwork before starting, and along the way.

The label states

“Appropriate laboratory tests to establish hematological and serum biochemical baseline data prior to, and periodically during, administration of any NSAID should be considered.”

Rimadyl (carprofen) label, Zoetis · Precautions

The prescription and the draws live on the same chart, dated — so you and your vet are reading the same story.

BarkLens quotes the label and attributes it. It sets no dose, interval or schedule — your vet does.

What BarkLens will not do

It does not diagnose. It does not screen. It does not tell you what’s wrong with your dog or predict what will happen to him.

It explains, it organizes, and it gives you the questions worth asking. Your vet makes the calls.

BarkLens uses AI and can make mistakes. That’s why every health claim points back to a source you can check yourself.
