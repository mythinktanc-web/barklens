Thyroid values in dogs

Dogs typically develop the opposite thyroid problem to cats: hypothyroidism, where the gland produces too little. The tests are straightforward. The interpretation is not.

Cornell’s Riney Canine Health Center states that more than 90% of dogs diagnosed with hypothyroidism have primary hypothyroidism.

Source · Cornell Riney Canine Health Center

Where BarkLens fits

Improvement is slow and uneven, which makes photos useful. Merck states that improvements in lethargy and mentation occur within two weeks of starting levothyroxine, while skin and coat changes and body weight may take up to three months. A dated photo from three months ago answers “is his coat actually better” in a way memory can’t.

Source · Merck — Hypothyroidism

Med timing relative to the blood draw matters. Keep the dose, the time given, and the draw date in one place, because the interpretation depends on the interval between them.

Cholesterol lives on the same line. It’s part of this picture (below), and Labs & Trends keeps it beside T4 instead of on a separate report from a different visit.

What the markers measure

Total T4 (TT4) — the main thyroid hormone. Often the first test run, and easily lowered by things that have nothing to do with the thyroid.

Free T4 (fT4) — the unbound, active portion. Less affected by other illness.

TSH — the signal from the pituitary telling the thyroid to work harder.

Why one low T4 isn’t the answer

The 2023 AAHA endocrinopathies guidelines state that an isolated total T4 below the reference interval should not be the only criterion used to diagnose hypothyroidism, because a reduction in TT4 can occur secondary to breed, advancing age, certain medications such as prednisone, and periods of illness or stress.

The same guidelines note that a TT4 result in the upper half of the reference range generally excludes the condition.

Source · 2023 AAHA Endocrinopathies Guidelines

Cornell’s diagnostic center states that measuring free T4 may be useful in distinguishing true hypothyroidism from reduced thyroid function caused by other chronic illness — such as Cushing’s, liver disease, or diabetes — by drug administration such as glucocorticoids, or by malnutrition.

Source · Cornell AHDC — Thyroid Testing

What’s read together

The 2023 AAHA guidelines state that a definitive diagnosis is characterized by TT4 and free T4 below the reference interval with TSH above it — however, approximately 20–40% of dogs with overt hypothyroidism have TSH within the reference range, so having two of the three hormone concentrations indicative of hypothyroidism can support the diagnosis.

Two of three. That is a rule about a combination, and it’s unreadable from any single value.

Why cholesterol is on a thyroid page

Merck states that the classic serum biochemical abnormality associated with hypothyroidism is high cholesterol, occurring in approximately 80% of affected dogs.

eClinPath notes that in dogs, hypothyroidism is associated with mild to marked elevations in cholesterol, and lists hypothyroidism among the common causes of high cholesterol without raised triglycerides, alongside nephrotic syndrome and cholestasis.

Source · Cornell eClinPath — Cholesterol

Merck also reports that the classic hematologic finding, in 40–50% of cases, is a non-regenerative anemia.

So a value on the cholesterol line and a value on the red cell line can both belong to a thyroid conversation. That is exactly the kind of connection that gets lost when reports live in separate emails.

The signs that usually prompt testing

Weight gain without more food. Lethargy. Coat and skin changes, sometimes symmetrical hair loss. Seeking out warm places. Recurrent skin or ear problems.

In the other direction, Merck notes that too much thyroid hormone is rare in dogs, and when it happens can cause increased drinking and urination, weight loss, panting, restlessness, or a fast heart rate. Worth knowing once he’s on treatment.

Questions worth asking your vet

·Could anything else he has going on be lowering this value?
·Would free T4 and TSH add anything to the total T4?
·Do any of his current medications affect thyroid testing?
·Does his cholesterol fit this picture?
·If we treat, how will we know it’s working, and when should I expect to see it?
