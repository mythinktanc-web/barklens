Heart disease in dogs

This is the condition where it matters most to say what bloodwork can’t do.

Bloodwork does not show a murmur, valve shape, chamber size, or rhythm. Heart disease in dogs is largely characterized by listening, imaging, and electrical recording. A normal blood panel is entirely compatible with significant heart disease — if your vet wants imaging rather than more blood, that’s usually why.

Where BarkLens fits

Counting breaths while he sleeps is a real clinical measure, and you’re the only one who can take it. Merck states that dogs with left-sided congestive heart failure often breathe faster than healthy dogs, and that sleeping respiratory rate can be regularly monitored to identify early heart failure and to assess response to treatment. Log it against the number your vet gives you — never a number from us.

Source · Merck — Heart Failure in Dogs

Several medications, tight schedules. The VETMEDIN label directs that the total daily dose be divided into two equal portions given approximately twelve hours apart, and notes it is indicated for use alongside concurrent therapy such as furosemide as appropriate case by case. Two or three drugs on different clocks is the normal state here — and if more than one person in the house is giving them, send a link and they join as a helper, free forever, so each dose shows who gave it and when.

Source · VETMEDIN label (DailyMed)

The kidney numbers are part of the heart picture. Merck’s guidance on ACE inhibitors recommends increasing to maximal dose with monitoring of kidney function, potassium, and blood pressure. Labs & Trends keeps creatinine and potassium beside the cardiac history rather than in a separate file.

Source · Merck — ACE Inhibitors

What blood tests can show

NT-proBNP — released when heart muscle is stretched. It can support a suspicion of heart disease and help judge whether breathing trouble is more likely cardiac or respiratory.

Cardiac troponin I — rises with heart muscle injury. Used in specific situations rather than routinely.

Kidney values and electrolytes — because heart medications affect them, and the two systems are closely linked.

Why heart disease is described in stages

The ACVIM consensus guidelines for myxomatous mitral valve disease describe Stage A as dogs at high risk of developing heart disease with no identifiable structural disorder — for example a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel without a murmur — and Stage B as dogs with structural disease, such as a typical murmur of mitral regurgitation, who have never developed clinical signs of heart failure.

The same guidelines describe Stage B1 as asymptomatic dogs without evidence of cardiac remodeling sufficient to meet criteria for starting treatment, and state that no drug or dietary treatment is recommended at Stage B1.

Source · ACVIM Consensus — MMVD

That last one is worth reading twice. “We’re not treating yet” can be the guideline-consistent answer, and knowing the staging is what lets you hear it as a plan rather than as inaction.

Which is also why the date a murmur was first heard, and what the last imaging showed, are worth having on hand.

A side effect you can actually observe

The FDA-approved label for Salix (furosemide) lists increased thirst among the early signs of electrolyte imbalance during therapy.

Source · SALIX label (DailyMed)

So “he’s drinking more since the new medication” is a reportable observation, not a stray detail — and it’s the kind of thing that’s easy to notice and then not mention three weeks later.

What owners are usually asked to record

Sleeping respiratory rate. Exercise tolerance. Coughing. Any fainting episode, with the date and what he’d been doing.

Questions worth asking your vet

·Does this need imaging rather than more bloodwork?
·Should I be counting his sleeping breathing rate, and what number would concern you?
·What stage would you put him at, and what changes at the next one?
·How do his kidney values interact with the heart medication?
·Which of these do I give together, and which have to be spaced?
·What would make you want to see him sooner?
